About

I'm in my third year of a BSc in Applied Physics at DCU, finishing top of my class with strongest results in nuclear physics, relativity, and electromagnetism. I like physics best when it's in front of me as hardware — a vacuum line that either holds pressure or doesn't, a sensor that either gives clean data or noise.

That's what pulled me toward surface science and instrumentation: over the past year I've spent most of my project time building a thermally programmed desorption system from the ground up, alongside a handful of smaller programming and electronics projects made mostly out of curiosity.

Research

Thermally Programmed Desorption System

Dublin City University · Jan – May 2026

An undergraduate research project built with a lecturer and a classmate: a working TPD rig for studying how molecules bind to and release from a surface as it's heated on a controlled ramp. I contributed 5–6 hours a week to the physical build.

  • Designed and assembled the vacuum chamber and pumping arrangement
  • Built and tuned the sample heating stage for controlled temperature ramps
  • Took the system through design, assembly, and testing as an ongoing iteration, not a one-shot build

A TPD spectrum — desorption rate plotted against temperature — is sketched above: each peak marks a binding site releasing its molecules at a characteristic temperature.
